1. Executive summary

LAUNCHPAD (consumer brand LAUNCHPADi; legal entity Lpad Intelligence Pvt Ltd) should position as Admissions Intelligence: helping families decide where they want admission, explore peer-tier alternatives (India + abroad + online), unlock finance/scholarship paths, and connect to people who know exact processes at target institutions.

Core thesis: Marks are necessary but not sufficient. Timing, process literacy, fit across peer-tier options, and funding exploration decide outcomes. Families often reject better-fit options as “too costly” without mapping loans, scholarships, sponsorships, or alternate destinations/tiers.

Today’s live site at www.launchpadi.com already states an admissions-intelligence narrative on the homepage, but the content surface is overwhelmingly MBBS-abroad / NEET SEO doorway pages (~179 .htm files). That creates a mismatch between brand promise and productized experience — and SEO risk from thin/near-duplicate country templates (documented in the internal SEO audit of 2026-07-21).

Strategic opportunity: Expand from “MBBS abroad consultant content” into a structured opportunity database + human network (consultants, institution process experts, finance partners) covering:

  • K–12 school admissions (local reputed schools, timelines, applications, references)
  • UG/PG higher education across medicine, engineering, law, and other fields
  • India + US/UK/Australia/Canada/Middle East/Europe/Asia + online programs
  • Finance: loans, scholarships, sponsorships — presented as decision tools, not afterthoughts

Competitive white space: Large portals (Shiksha, Collegedunia, CollegeDekho) win discovery SEO but weak high-trust strategy. Study-abroad specialists (Yocket, Leap, Leverage, IDP, AECC, AdmitKard) monetize Big-4/Europe pathways and university commissions. School consultants (e.g. Schoolling) are city-deep but narrow. MBBS-abroad agents own Russia/Georgia/Central Asia SEO but often lack finance transparency and India alternatives. Few players own the full “marks → fit → funding → process expert” loop across school + college + abroad.

Website planning goal: Replace doorway sprawl with hubs, opportunity cards, journey tools, and inquiry routing — then deepen categories and partner networks in phases. Do not rebuild the live marketing site in this document; this is the blueprint.

3. Industry landscape

India’s admissions economy is not one market. It is stacked segments that families move through over 15+ years — often with different vendors each time (school agent → coaching → college portal → study-abroad counsellor → loan broker).

3.1 Size drivers (use carefully — mixed definitions)

  • Outbound student stock (official): MEA data tabled in Parliament (2025) reports 1,882,318 Indians studying abroad across 153 countries — including, for the first time, 628,305 school-level enrolments and 1,254,013 in universities/tertiary. Higher-education enrolments fell ~5.7% vs 2024’s ~1.33M after three years of growth — attributed by press analysis to visa/immigration policy shifts in major destinations. Sources: Indian Express, ICEF Monitor, The PIE News.
  • Spend pool (industry commentary, not official GDP series): Commentary often cites ~$85B expected spend by Indian students on overseas education (tuition + living + services) as the commercial “pie” study-abroad firms chase. Treat as estimate / secondary (e.g. LinkedIn industry overview citing HolonIQ-style framing).
  • “India education consulting” market reports: Market Research Future lists India education consulting at roughly USD 189M (2024) with modest long-range CAGRs depending on which MRFR table is cited — and profiles large strategy firms (McKinsey, Deloitte, etc.), so this figure is not a clean outbound-agent TAM. Independent analysis (RAOSCAFF) notes no reliable primary FICCI/CII/Redseer figure for India outbound-only consultancy revenue. Do not use MRFR alone as Launchpad’s addressable market.
  • University commission economics (secondary survey claim): Industry write-ups cite average commissions on the order of USD 2,500–3,500 per successfully placed student for commission-based agencies — useful as a model clue, not Launchpad revenue forecast.

3.3 Pain points Launchpad can own

  1. Single-path tunnel vision — “Only IIT / only AIIMS / only Canada” without peer-tier maps.
  2. Cost rejection without finance exploration — sticker price ≠ net payable.
  3. Process opacity — counselling rounds, CAF points, visa checklists, NMC duration rules.
  4. Conflicted advice — commission-led destination push vs family goals.
  5. Fragmented vendors — no continuity from school → college → abroad.

3.4 Regulatory notes (non-exhaustive; verify before counselling claims)

  • NEET / NMC / FMGL: Indian students intending to practice allopathy in India after foreign UG medical study must meet NMC Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate conditions (duration, English medium, internship, etc.). NMC has repeatedly alerted students about foreign private colleges not aligned with FMGL (e.g. UGMEB advisories). NEET qualification is required for eligibility pathways; FMGE (and evolving NExT policy) sits on the return-to-practice path. See NMC advisories and explainers in Appendix.
  • India seat allotment: MCC / state counselling (NEET UG), JoSAA/CSAB (engineering), state CAP rounds, CLAT consortium processes — official portals are source of truth; consultants add navigation, not substitute authority.
  • Study-abroad agents: Destination regulators (UK Agent Quality Framework / British Council training, ICEF, AIRC, Australian ESOS ecosystem) increasingly pressure quality and transparency. Claiming “guaranteed admission/visa” is both unethical and a brand risk.
  • School admissions: City rules (e.g. Delhi nursery points / CAF) change; local process experts matter more than national SEO content.

4. Opportunity taxonomy / category map

Exhaustive structured list of categories Launchpad can cover over time. MVP should pick 2–3 depth wedges; the taxonomy is the product roadmap north star.

4.1 K–12 school admissions

  • Nursery / pre-primary; primary; middle; secondary / senior secondary
  • Board types: CBSE, ICSE/ISC, state boards, IB, IGCSE, Cambridge
  • School tiers: neighbourhood strong schools → city-reputed → national brand / international
  • Services: shortlisting, points/probability, CAF & docs, essays, interviews, waitlist strategy, mid-year / transfer, boarding
  • Geography hubs: Delhi NCR first (proven demand), then Mumbai, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Chennai, Pune, Kolkata

4.2 Undergraduate (India)

  • Medicine & allied: MBBS, BDS, AYUSH, nursing, physiotherapy, pharmacy
  • Engineering & tech: B.Tech / BE, specialized computing, design-adjacent
  • Law: 5-year integrated, 3-year LLB
  • Business: BBA, BMS, IPM pathways
  • Pure sciences, arts, commerce, design, hospitality, agriculture, architecture
  • Path types: government merit, state quota, All-India, private / management / NRI quotas (ethics & transparency mandatory), CUET central universities, deemed universities

4.3 Postgraduate (India)

  • Medical PG (NEET PG / INI-CET), engineering M.Tech, MBA/PGDM, LLM, M.Des, research tracks
  • Career switchers and working-professional programs

4.4 Abroad destinations (higher ed)

  • Traditional Big-4: US, UK, Canada, Australia
  • Europe rising hubs: Germany, Ireland, France, Netherlands, Italy, Spain, etc.
  • Middle East: UAE and regional university cities (incl. school-level diaspora demand)
  • Asia: Singapore, Malaysia, Japan, South Korea, etc.
  • Medical-abroad corridors: Russia, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Philippines, Egypt, Eastern Europe — with NMC-compliance intelligence as differentiator

4.5 Online / hybrid / transnational

  • UGC-entitled online degrees, foreign online / distance caveats, twinning / 2+2, pathway colleges
  • Position as fit & recognition advisory, not commodity upsell

4.6 Finance & access

  • Education loans (secured / unsecured), co-borrower strategy
  • Scholarships (merit, need, destination, diaspora, institutional)
  • Sponsorships / employer / CSR / community funding
  • Cost-of-attendance calculators (tuition + living + insurance + travel)
  • Remittance & forex hygiene (partner, not core product)

4.7 Test-prep adjacency (differentiated)

Launchpad is admissions strategy, not a coaching replacement. Adjacent offerings:

  • Exam calendars & “what score unlocks which tier”
  • LAUNCHPADi-Q as readiness / practice utility
  • Referrals to prep partners (affiliate) when strategy says “raise score first”

4.8 Network / marketplace objects

  • Verified consultants (by city, stream, destination)
  • Institution process experts / alumni / faculty contacts (privacy-preserving)
  • University / school partnership inventory
  • Loan & scholarship partners

5. Competitor research

Ten named competitors researched via live site fetches / public pages (July 2026). Analysis focuses on positioning, site IA/content, and business-model clues — not financial diligence.

1. Leverage Edu

https://www.leverageedu.com/

Positioning
AI-native “global talent mobility & upskilling” across study, work, and online — far beyond classic counselling. Includes Study Global, Work Global (nursing/teaching/vocational), Ivy100, Leverage MBBS, Leverage Pathways.
Target segment
Ambitious Indian (and global) students/professionals; also B2B consultant partners (claims 10K+ education consultants / white-label AI).
Website & content
Heavy product marketing, AI coach CTA, success stories, destination modules. Content engine via Learn/blog historically SEO-strong. CTA: talk to AI coach / counselling. Strength: breadth + brand gravity. Weakness: noise — admissions strategy can feel secondary to “mobility platform” ambition.
Business model clues
University recruitment + counselling; expansion into finance, housing, careers; partner/white-label; press historically cited large revenue claims (treat press figures cautiously).
vs Launchpad
Too broad/capital-intensive to copy. Launchpad wins by clarity of admissions decisioning + school-to-college continuity + finance-before-rejection — not by matching AI-mobility theater.

2. Yocket

https://yocket.com/

Positioning
“Confirmed admits” study-abroad platform; Premium counselling + Prep (GRE/GMAT/IELTS/TOEFL) + Finance (30+ lenders).
Target segment
MS/MBA/Bachelor’s/PhD aspirants for US/UK/Canada/Germany and similar.
Website & content
Strong conversion IA: product pillars, admit stats, acceptance-rate comparison widgets, 1:1 expert CTA, app download. Community/content historically SEO-relevant for “university shortlist” queries. Strength: clear paid Premium ladder. Weakness: little India domestic or K–12; medicine-abroad not core brand.
Business model clues
Paid premium counselling; test-prep product; loan referral; free top-of-funnel community/content. (LeapScholar footer lists Yocket partnership — ecosystem consolidation signal.)
vs Launchpad
Compete only if Launchpad enters Big-4 profile-building. Differentiator: Launchpad’s peer-tier + India options + medical NMC intelligence + schools.

3. LeapScholar (Leap)

7. Business structure / operating model

7.1 Value chain

  1. Demand — parents/students arrive via SEO hubs, referrals, YouTube, WhatsApp, partner schools/coaching.
  2. Intake — profile (stage, academics, budget band, geography, constraints, timeline).
  3. Strategize — peer-tier opportunity set (reach / match / safety) across India, abroad, online; finance overlay.
  4. Match — route to Launchpad counsellor and/or specialized consultant / institution process expert.
  5. Execute — applications, counselling rounds, docs, visas as applicable.
  6. Outcomes — admits, joins, funding closed; feedback into opportunity database.

7.2 Revenue hypotheses (directions — not forecasts)

StreamWho paysNotes
Strategy consult fees Parent / student Schoolling-like packages; high trust when priced transparently
Success / placement fees Parent or institution Disclose clearly; avoid double-dipping without consent
University / school partner leads Institution Portal model; protect “neutral mode”
Study-abroad commissions University / pathway Industry norms often cited ~USD 2.5–3.5k per place (secondary); varies widely
Finance referral Lender / partner Attach after fit decision — brand-safe sequencing
LAUNCHPADi-Q / tools User or B2B Secondary; supports readiness narrative
Consultant marketplace take-rate Consultant Only after supply density; start private matching

Do not invent revenue targets until pipeline experiments produce conversion data. Public competitor “₹X crore” headlines are marketing, not diligence.

  • Partner graph (consultants, lenders, universities) with compliance tags

8.2 User journeys

  1. Discover — land on hub/guide from search or social; understand Launchpad’s non-coaching stance.
  2. Explore — filter opportunities by stage, stream, budget band, geography, online/on-campus.
  3. Strategize — short profile → peer-tier shortlist (reach/match/safety) + finance flags.
  4. Inquire — form / WhatsApp / calendar; consent + urgency + preferred contact window.
  5. Human follow-up — counsellor assigns path; optionally introduces specialist consultant.

8.3 Content types to present

  • Hub pages — one strong URL per country/stream/city (merge doorways).
  • Opportunity cards — structured fields; compare; save; inquire.
  • Process playbooks — checklists with official source links.
  • Finance explainers — calculators + partner CTAs after fit.
  • Timelines / calendars — NEET, JoSAA, school CAF windows, intake months abroad.
  • Trust / E-E-A-T — about, methodology, last-reviewed dates, no “guaranteed seat” claims, author/reviewer where possible.

8.4 Inquiry / CRM flow

  • Primary: multi-step web form (stage → goals → budget band → timeline → contact).
  • Secondary: WhatsApp Business deep link with prefilled context.
  • Tertiary: calendar booking for strategy calls (once staffing ready).
  • Routing: tags by segment (K–12 / NEET / abroad / finance) → owner queue; SLA response.
  • CRM fields: source page, UTM, consent, partner-of-interest, disclosure flags.

8.5 Consultants / partners on site

Recommendation: Start with private matching (Launchpad routes quietly). Public directory only after vetting standards, dispute process, and enough supply — otherwise thin directories hurt trust. Partner apply page can exist early.

8.6 SEO strategy shift

From: 3–5 near-duplicate MBBS doorway URLs per country.
To: Priority country hubs with unique evidence + NMC notes + fees tables with year/source + internal links to finance and India alternatives; noindex or 301 thin siblings; strengthen About/methodology; integrate Q and inquire CTAs mid-content.

Aligns with existing internal SEO audit recommendations (merge country clusters; keep NEET/fees comparison/FMGE/loan guides; kill doorway spam patterns).
